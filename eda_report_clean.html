1. Dataset Structure & Relationships

The dataset spans six interrelated tables. This is a 20% representative sample — class ratios and distributions are preserved.

Table Rows Description Key
customers.csv 39,988 Demographics, KYC flags, banking registrations customer_id
accounts.csv 40,038 Account attributes, balance metrics, status account_id
transactions (×6 parts) 7,424,845 Every transaction — channel, amount, counterparty account_id
customer_account_linkage.csv 40,038 Bridge: maps customers → accounts customer_id, account_id
product_details.csv 39,988 Product holdings: loans, credit cards, overdraft customer_id
train_labels.csv 24,023 Ground truth: is_mule flag, flag date, alert reason account_id
test_accounts.csv 16,015 Accounts to predict on in Phase 2 account_id

Join path:
customers → (customer_id) → linkage → (account_id) → accounts → transactions
customers → (customer_id) → product_details
accounts → (account_id) → train_labels / test_accounts

Note: There is no direct customer_id in accounts.csv — you must route through customer_account_linkage. After joining, the master training table has 24,023 rows × 60 columns.

Key channels decoded: UPC/UPD (UPI credit/debit ~66% of all transactions), IPM (IMPS — inter-bank instant), NTD (NEFT debit), FTD/FTC (fund transfers), ATW (ATM withdrawal — physical cash), STD (standing instruction — recurring payments), END (e-commerce/POS), P2A (pay-to-account). 34 channels total exist in the dataset — the remainder appear in under 1% of transactions each.

2. The Class Imbalance Problem

Class Count % of Training Set
Legitimate 23,760 98.91%
Mule 263 1.09%

Imbalance ratio: 90:1. A model always predicting "legit" gets 98.91% accuracy — but is completely useless. AUC-ROC is the correct evaluation metric.

⚠️ In Phase 2, SMOTE oversampling or class-weight balancing will be applied.

Key interpretations:

  • Pass-through rate (7.53% vs 0%): Mule accounts function as temporary fund conduits, not personal savings accounts.
  • Unique counterparties (30 vs 10): Consistent with fan-in/fan-out laundering — aggregating from many or distributing to many.
  • ATM withdrawal (1.69% vs 0%): Physical cash extraction is the final step — converting digital funds to untraceable cash.
  • Standing instruction (0% vs 1.10%): Absence of recurring payments signals no stable financial life — accounts exist solely to move money.
  • Round amount % (11.5% vs 16.78%): Mules move precise amounts received from others — round numbers are a normal-user behaviour.
  • Customer age (49.9 vs 49.5): Demographics offer no signal — mule recruitment cuts across all age groups equally.

4a. Temporal Burst Behaviour

Burst ratio measures the peak-to-median monthly transaction count per account — a proxy for episodic fund movement intensity.

Mule accounts exhibit significantly higher burst ratios, reflecting episodic, concentrated fund movement rather than steady financial usage. Accounts remain dormant between activation episodes — consistent with short-window cycling.

The Cliff's delta effect size confirms practical separation beyond statistical significance alone. Burst ratio captures temporal irregularity that transactional aggregates (total count, average amount) miss.

4b. Network Structure Analysis

Counterparty entropy and volume concentration quantify how broadly (or narrowly) an account distributes its transaction partners and funds.

Mule accounts show higher counterparty entropy — transaction partners are distributed more evenly across a wider set, rather than concentrated among a few regular relationships. Their pct_volume_top3 is correspondingly lower, confirming funds are not channelled through a small trusted group.

This combination is consistent with fan-in / fan-out laundering: funds aggregate from many sources and disperse to many destinations. The large Cliff's delta effect sizes for both metrics make network structure the dominant behavioural signal for mule detection.

4c. Branch-Level Risk Assessment

Branches with fewer than 30 accounts are excluded to control small-sample inflation.

Most branches cluster near RR ≈ 1 (global baseline). A small number show elevated relative risk (RR > 2), warranting further investigation.

Caution: Elevated branch risk does not imply intentional staff collusion. It may reflect geographic clustering, local customer demographics, or coincidental concentration. Branch risk is best used as a contextual feature, not a standalone flag.

6a. Top Behavioural Predictors by Empirical Separation

Features ranked by absolute Cliff's delta — a non-parametric effect size measuring practical separation between mule and legitimate distributions.

Network structure metrics — counterparty entropy and volume concentration — dominate the ranking. These capture the fan-in/fan-out topology distinguishing mule pipelines from personal accounts.

Temporal burst ratio provides moderate separation. Salary window percentage shows weak discriminatory power in this sample.

What Did Not Show Predictive Signal

The following features showed no meaningful separation despite theoretical justification:

  • Salary window % (pct_txn_day_1_5) — transaction timing relative to salary dates
  • Longest inactivity gap — dormancy periods before activation
  • Active span days — total account activity duration
  • Customer age — no demographic signal (Section 3)
  • Round amount % — structuring hypothesis unsupported (Section 3)

Null findings are equally important — they narrow the feature space and prevent overfitting to noise.

7. Key Takeaways & Phase 2 Direction

Finding Detail
Top behavioural signal Pass-through — money in/out same day (7.53% vs 0%)
Strongest predictor Frozen status (40% vs 2%) — use with leakage caution
Novel finding ATM withdrawals (1.69% vs 0%) — cash extraction endpoint
Null findings Age, tenure, dormancy, round amounts — no signal
Temporal gap suspicious_start/end window analysis deferred to Phase 2 — burst_ratio partially captures this

The mule account profile: High transaction velocity, large amounts, wide counterparty networks, same-day fund cycling — a financial pipeline, not a personal account.

Phase 2 approach: Gradient boosted tree (XGBoost/LightGBM) on 20+ features with class-weight balancing. Metric: AUC-ROC.

✅ Bottom line: Features capturing pipeline behaviour — pass-through rate, counterparty breadth, channel mix, and balance-to-transaction ratio — will drive a high-AUC model.
